Research focus

My MS thesis at NUST tackled rotor-failure mitigation: when a quadcopter loses a rotor, the controller reconfigures it to fly as a tricopter. That work grew into a broader interest in controllers that stay stable when hardware fails, and in tuning them with optimisation instead of trial and error.

At NASTP I now work on the systems side: autopilot integration, telemetry routing, fail-safe logic and flight-envelope testing on Cube Orange and Pixhawk platforms.

Selected work

Research · SEECS, NUST · 2023–2025

Nonlinear control for rotor-failure recovery

Flight controllers for bicopter, tricopter-transition, quadcopter and hexacopter platforms that hold attitude and heading and recover from a lost rotor.

  • Designed SMC, Super-Twisting SMC, Terminal SMC, impulsive control and ITST-SMC.
  • Tuned controller gains with RedFox Optimization and Particle Swarm Optimization.
  • Validated in MATLAB/Simulink, then controller-in-the-loop and hardware-in-the-loop.
  • Result: faster settling, lower tracking error and stable recovery after rotor failure.

Tethered UAV power system

A long-endurance multicopter powered from the ground, built to get past the flight-time limit of battery-only drones.

  • Transmitted 220 V AC up the tether to an airborne 1000 W AC-DC converter.
  • Added a LiPo backup so the aircraft can recover safely if tether power drops.
  • Analysed power budget, tether resistance and voltage drop, cable loss, heat, tether mass and payload trade-offs.
  • Demonstrated as an official exhibitor at IDEAS 2024, Karachi.
